What am I looking at?

Who's being watched

This tool captures on your Mac's network interfaces. That means it sees traffic this Mac sends or receives — nothing else. Other phones and laptops on your wifi are invisible from here, because wifi hands each device only its own traffic. To watch a whole network, the tool would need to run on the router (or receive a mirrored copy of router traffic).

How devices are identified

🍎
Apple — vendor from MAC prefix
📱
Android / other phone
💻
Computer (Intel, Dell, Lenovo, etc.)
💡
IoT / smart-home device
🖥️
Unknown — no MAC visible (e.g. VPN tunnel, only IP)

The MAC address tells us the manufacturer, not the model. On a VPN interface there is no MAC, so we key by IP and can't guess the device type.

What each event means

🔐
Login attempt — traffic to a dedicated identity endpoint (accounts.google.com, login.microsoftonline.com, Okta, etc.). Almost always an actual sign-in in progress.
🛡️
Two-factor challenge — Duo or similar MFA endpoint hit during the login flow.
🌐
Site visit — the device loaded a site (Slack, GitHub, LinkedIn, Figma…). Could be a login, could be normal use — we can't tell the difference because the hostname is shared. Login rows for the same provider (e.g. Google) stay separate from visit rows.

What we can't see

No passwords, no tokens, no payload content — all of that is inside TLS and stays inside TLS. We only read the hostname in the connection setup (the SNI field) and DNS queries. That's the same metadata Wireshark or your router would see.

Interfaces & VPN

en0 / en1 are physical interfaces (wifi / ethernet). utun* is a VPN tunnel. When a VPN is on, real traffic goes through the utun; the en0 side just shows an encrypted blob to the VPN server. That's why we sniff both.
